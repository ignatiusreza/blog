_Last updated: October 2026._

### Still in Berlin, still working on Laboperator

Six years on, I'm still working on [Laboperator](https://www.laboperator.com/), though the company around it has changed. I joined it at Labforward. Since then it has moved to Xavo, and today the team is part of LabAthena. The mission hasn't changed: a plug & play platform that connects all of a lab's instruments, collects their data automatically and automates the devices themselves, so scientists can spend their time on science.

Most of my days are spent on the device side of that: the connectors and drivers that talk to the instruments, and keeping that fleet modern and healthy. I'm still not a scientist, and I still find it exciting that something I helped build might sit somewhere in the path of a discovery.

### Building with agentic coding tools

Most of the code I've shipped this year, I didn't type. I described it, read it, argued with it and sent it back. It's changed how I work more than any tool since I started programming, and it's changed which ideas I bother trying. I've been [writing about what that means](/articles/above-and-below-the-code) for the people who build software.

### Making things for fun again

That cheaper path from idea to prototype finally got me to build [Voice Runner](/projects/voice-runner), an endless runner whose stages are generated from whatever music is playing around you, and which you steer by shouting at it. It had been sitting in my head for a year. More small playgrounds like it are likely to show up here.

### Writing again

After more than a decade of silence, this blog is alive again. Lately I've been writing about how we talk to computers ([Say the Magic Word](/articles/say-the-magic-word)), about building software people actually want to use ([Technology for the People](/articles/technology-for-the-people)), and about where the work goes once typing code stops being the job ([Above and Below the Code](/articles/above-and-below-the-code)).

### Should I write a book or two?

I still have a couple of book ideas I keep coming back to. Should I write them?

- What If: There's no Sport

  Unpopular opinion, but I'm not a big proponent of sports in general. The World Cup, the Olympics and the like don't excite me. Which got me thinking: what if they never existed in the first place? How different would the world be?

- The Origin of Things - What Were They Thinking?

  I'm sure that at one point or another, you've wondered things like: what was the first person who discovered that cow's milk is drinkable thinking?
